Chess of the Angels

An original chess variant for the era of human–AI collaboration.

Can machines think? And if they can — can they be beaten?

The challenge

Chess is a finite, computable game — theoretically solvable, but not yet solved. What has been achieved is something more insidious: AI engines have accumulated so much knowledge that human intuition, creativity and experience no longer constitute a meaningful advantage.

Chess of the Angels was designed in response. Its conditional activation mechanic introduces structural uncertainty that no engine can fully anticipate — because it depends entirely on the opponent's own decisions.

The objective

To restore a fair and balanced competition between human and machine. Chess of the Angels introduces a new logical dimension — not more pieces, not a bigger board, but a new type of conditioned causality — that allows human intuition and creativity to compete on equal terms with artificial intelligence.

The variant also enables a new form of collaboration: human and AI can explore together a space where neither holds an accumulated advantage, making it a unique field for joint research, learning and discovery.

About the Work

Chess of the Angels preserves the objective and foundations of classical chess while introducing a 68-square board, four lateral Angel Wing squares and two new bicolour pieces per side — the Angels.

The Angels begin inactive. They acquire their movement and control when an opposing piece completes a legal move on the territorial frontier. Activation is collective, permanent and irreversible.

The Official Board

Why This Variant Matters

Expanded state space

The 68-square board and activation mechanic expand the state space by several orders of magnitude beyond classical chess.

Structural uncertainty

A position is not fully described by what is visible — it also depends on whether the Angels have been activated. No prior engine knowledge applies.

AI research value

Agents learning this variant from scratch must develop entirely new evaluation functions — a productive benchmark for reinforcement learning.

Human creativity restored

The initiative–response mechanic rewards anticipation and strategic patience in ways that pure calculation cannot replicate.
