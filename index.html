Welcome

Chess of the Angels

A chess variant designed for the era of human–AI collaboration.

Can machines think? If they cannot, what makes them so difficult to beat?

The challenge

Chess is a finite, computable game — theoretically solvable, but not yet solved. What has been achieved is something more insidious: AI engines have accumulated so much knowledge that human intuition, creativity and experience no longer constitute a meaningful advantage.

Chess of the Angels was designed in response. Its conditional activation mechanic introduces structural uncertainty that no engine can fully anticipate — because it depends entirely on the opponent's own decisions.

The objective

To restore a fair and balanced competition between human and machine. Chess of the Angels introduces a new logical dimension — not more pieces, not a bigger board, but a new type of conditioned causality — that allows human intuition and creativity to compete on equal terms with artificial intelligence.

The variant also enables a new form of collaboration: human and AI can explore together a space where neither holds an accumulated advantage, making it a unique field for joint research, learning and discovery.

About Chess of the Angels

Chess of the Angels was created from a simple question: can chess continue to evolve in an era where artificial intelligence has surpassed human players?

For centuries, chess has represented one of humanity's greatest intellectual achievements — combining mathematical calculation, logical reasoning, creativity, intuition and long-term strategic thinking within a remarkably elegant system of rules. Today, however, elite competition increasingly relies on preparation assisted by artificial intelligence. Modern engines analyse millions of positions every second and benefit from decades of accumulated opening theory, endgame databases and computer-generated knowledge.

Chess of the Angels was conceived as a response to this new reality. Rather than replacing classical chess, it seeks to expand it. The game preserves the familiar foundations of traditional chess while introducing an entirely new strategic environment: a 68-square board, four Angel Wing squares, two Angel pieces per player and a unique conditional activation mechanism.

Unlike every traditional chess piece, an Angel does not possess the same abilities throughout the entire game. It begins inactive and only becomes fully operational when the opposing player crosses the territorial frontier. A strategic decision made by one player permanently changes the possibilities available to the other. This simple rule transforms the decision tree of the game.

In classical chess every piece has a fixed set of legal moves from the beginning of the game until the end. Chess of the Angels introduces a new concept: state-dependent pieces. The capabilities of an Angel depend on the evolving history of the position rather than on geometry alone. Every territorial advance must be evaluated not only for the space it gains, but also for the strategic opportunities it grants the opponent.

The objective is not to make chess random. The objective is to reward creativity over memorisation, strategic adaptation over repetition, and original thinking over accumulated database knowledge.

For this reason, Chess of the Angels is also an interesting research platform for artificial intelligence. The variant provides a new environment for studying search algorithms, evaluation functions, reinforcement learning and human–AI collaboration — without relying on centuries of existing opening theory.

Every game begins as unexplored territory. Every position invites new ideas. Every invasion carries consequences.

The Official Board

File order from White's left: X, a, b, c, d, e, f, g, h, Y. The Angel is abbreviated G.

Registered EU design: 68-square board — EUIPO No. 015110333-0001

We took the classical 8×8 chessboard and asked: what if four more squares existed — two on each side, exactly where the Angels begin? The result is a 68-square board. The central grid works exactly as in classical chess. The four new squares — x4, x5, y4 and y5 — are called the Angel Wings. They are real squares: any piece can enter, block or capture on them.

We also created a boundary. Ranks 4 and 5 form the Frontier — the line that separates the two territories. When an opposing piece crosses your Frontier, your Angels wake up. That is the heart of the game.

We designed a piece that does not attack first. The Angel starts asleep — it occupies its square, blocks lines, but cannot move or control. It waits.

When the opponent crosses the Frontier, the Angel activates. From that moment it moves exactly 3 squares in a straight line — horizontal, vertical or diagonal — as long as both intermediate squares are free. It cannot capture traditional pieces. It controls the square it can reach, even if occupied. If the king stands on a controlled square, it is in check.

One more rule: before the game begins, Black moves one Angel 3 squares toward the centre. This is the preliminary opening — mandatory, simple, and it does not activate anything. It is Black's way of balancing White's first-move advantage.
