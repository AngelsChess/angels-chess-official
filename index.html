Welcome

Chess of the Angels

A chess variant designed for the era of human–AI collaboration.

The challenge

Chess is a finite, computable game — theoretically solvable, but not yet solved. What has been achieved is something more insidious: AI engines have accumulated so much knowledge that human intuition, creativity and experience no longer constitute a meaningful advantage.

Chess of the Angels was designed in response. Its conditional activation mechanic introduces structural uncertainty that no engine can fully anticipate — because it depends entirely on the opponent's own decisions.

The objective

To restore a fair and balanced competition between human and machine. Chess of the Angels introduces a new logical dimension — not more pieces, not a bigger board, but a new type of conditioned causality — that allows human intuition and creativity to compete on equal terms with artificial intelligence.

The variant also enables a new form of collaboration: human and AI can explore together a space where neither holds an accumulated advantage, making it a unique field for joint research, learning and discovery.

About Chess of the Angels

Chess of the Angels was created from a simple question: can chess continue to evolve in an era where artificial intelligence has surpassed human players?

For centuries, chess has represented one of humanity's greatest intellectual achievements — combining mathematical calculation, logical reasoning, creativity, intuition and long-term strategic thinking within a remarkably elegant system of rules. Today, however, elite competition increasingly relies on preparation assisted by artificial intelligence. Modern engines analyse millions of positions every second and benefit from decades of accumulated opening theory, endgame databases and computer-generated knowledge.

Chess of the Angels was conceived as a response to this new reality. Rather than replacing classical chess, it seeks to expand it. The game preserves the familiar foundations of traditional chess while introducing an entirely new strategic environment: a 68-square board, four Angel Wing squares, two Angel pieces per player and a unique conditional activation mechanism.

Unlike every traditional chess piece, an Angel does not possess the same abilities throughout the entire game. It begins inactive and only becomes fully operational when the opposing player crosses the territorial frontier. A strategic decision made by one player permanently changes the possibilities available to the other. This simple rule transforms the decision tree of the game.

In classical chess every piece has a fixed set of legal moves from the beginning of the game until the end. Chess of the Angels introduces a new concept: state-dependent pieces. The capabilities of an Angel depend on the evolving history of the position rather than on geometry alone. Every territorial advance must be evaluated not only for the space it gains, but also for the strategic opportunities it grants the opponent.

The objective is not to make chess random. The objective is to reward creativity over memorisation, strategic adaptation over repetition, and original thinking over accumulated database knowledge.

For this reason, Chess of the Angels is also an interesting research platform for artificial intelligence. The variant provides a new environment for studying search algorithms, evaluation functions, reinforcement learning and human–AI collaboration — without relying on centuries of existing opening theory.

Every game begins as unexplored territory. Every position invites new ideas. Every invasion carries consequences.

The Official Board

File order from White's left: X, a, b, c, d, e, f, g, h, Y. The Angel is abbreviated G.

Registered EU design: 68-square board — EUIPO No. 015110333-0001

We took the classical 8×8 chessboard and asked: what if four more squares existed — two on each side, exactly where the Angels begin? The result is a 68-square board. The central grid works exactly as in classical chess. The four new squares — x4, x5, y4 and y5 — are called the Angel Wings. They are real squares: any piece can enter, block or capture on them.

We also created a boundary. Ranks 4 and 5 form the Frontier — the line that separates the two territories. When an opposing piece crosses your Frontier, your Angels wake up. That is the heart of the game.

The Angel Pieces

Each player begins with 18 pieces: 16 traditional plus 2 Angels.

Registered EU designs:
Black Angel — EUIPO No. 015111340-0001
White Angel — EUIPO No. 015111340-0002

We designed a piece that does not attack first. The Angel starts asleep — it occupies its square, blocks lines, but cannot move or control. It waits. This is not a weakness. It is a design decision: a piece whose power depends entirely on what the opponent chooses to do.

When the opponent crosses the Frontier, the Angel activates. From that moment it moves exactly 3 squares in a straight line — horizontal, vertical or diagonal — as long as both intermediate squares are free. It cannot capture traditional pieces. It controls the square it can reach, even if occupied. If the king stands on a controlled square, it is in check.

This creates what we call the unexpected node: a moment in the game tree where a move that seemed purely offensive — crossing the Frontier — suddenly generates new defensive possibilities for the opponent. The position branches in a way that no engine trained on classical chess can fully anticipate. The invasion triggers something that did not exist before.

One more rule: before the game begins, Black moves one Angel 3 squares toward the centre. This is the preliminary opening — mandatory, simple, and it does not activate anything. It is Black's way of balancing White's first-move advantage.

If you love chess, this game was made for you. The board will feel familiar. The rules are few. But the moment an Angel activates — and the territory shifts — you will understand why we built it. Come and play.

Why Chess of the Angels?

From game design and computational theory

1. The end of accumulated opening theory

Modern chess has been deeply theorised by computers. Stockfish, Leela Chess Zero and AlphaZero have analysed millions of games and generated opening knowledge that extends to move 30 or beyond. A piece that generates unexpected nodes renders that accumulated knowledge obsolete from move 1, forcing both players — human and machine — to reason from basic strategic principles rather than retrieving memorised lines.

2. The collapse of traditional chess engines

Engines based on alpha-beta pruning and brute-force calculation face a combinatorial explosion when confronted with the Angel. But the problem is not merely one of scale: the search tree ceases to be static. The value of each node depends on a variable external to the board — the activation state of the Angels — which did not exist in classical chess. The game rewards human intuition and psychological flexibility over deep, rigid calculation.

3. Structural uncertainty: the permanent Plan B

Classical chess is a perfect abstract war: all information is visible, there is no randomness, no fog. The Angel introduces fog in a structural, not random, way. Every territorial advance activates resources that previously did not exist. The invading player knows what they are doing — but cannot calculate exactly what position will result from that activation in the future. Plan B ceases to be an option and becomes a permanent strategic necessity.

4. Asymmetric information and deferred causality

In classical chess all information is on the board at every moment — it is a game of perfect information. The Angel introduces something qualitatively new: a variable whose moment of change depends on a future decision by the opponent. This is not random uncertainty. It is strategic uncertainty: the invading player knows they will activate the opponent's Angels, but cannot know what exact consequences that activation will have on a position that does not yet exist. That has never occurred before in the history of chess.

A message from the future

Chess of the Angels was not born to compete with classical chess. It was born to extend it.

When AlphaZero demonstrated in 2017 that a system capable of learning from scratch — without prior knowledge, without opening theory, without databases — could surpass Stockfish, the most powerful engine trained over decades, in just a few hours, the world witnessed something that transcended chess: proof that pure learning can overcome accumulated knowledge.

Chess of the Angels carries that same logic to the physical board. Every game begins from zero. No engine accumulates advantage. No database helps. The player who knows how to read the moment of activation — who understands when to invade and when to wait — holds an advantage that no algorithm can purchase with calculation time.

This is the board of the future: not larger, not faster, but structurally more honest. A board where human intelligence and artificial intelligence can sit face to face, without either arriving with the answer already prepared. The game that awaits them both.

A game that belongs to everyone

Chess has no borders. Each language on this site carries a different history of the same game.

RU — Russian

Where the chessboard is a philosophical battlefield and the cradle of a perpetual dynasty of intellectual titans. In 1824, Aleksandr Petrov — considered the first great Russian chess master — published the first chess manual in the Russian Empire: Chess Game, Systematically Analysed, in Saint Petersburg.

EN — English

The language that universalised the game, marked by the disruptive and solitary genius of Bobby Fischer.

NO — Norwegian

The mirror of the modern era, where the intuition and absolute coldness of Magnus Carlsen redefined human limits.

ES — Spanish

The Spanish-speaking world carried out the most revolutionary cultural transformation in the modern history of chess. At the court of the Catholic Monarchs in the late 15th century, the slow old piece of the alferes became the all-powerful Queen, accelerating the aesthetic aggression of the game forever. The first printed book on modern chess — Repetición de Amores y Arte de Ajedrez by Luis Ramírez de Lucena (1497) — introduced the current rules with the Queen and the modern board.

中文 — Chinese

Millennial patience and collective discipline projected toward the absolute domination of today's elite.

VI — Vietnamese

The vibrant energy and tactical sharpness of a new powerhouse transforming Southeast Asia.

ՀՅ — Armenian

A nation where chess is not a pastime, but a form of national identity and intellectual survival.

FR — French

Classic tradition and strategic refinement that shaped Western chess thought.

IS — Icelandic

Geographical isolation turned into mysticism, eternal witness to the legendary geopolitical clash of 1972.

TR — Turkish

The silent sowing of new generations building the future of the board.

日本語 — Japanese

The technological bridge between millennial Eastern tactical tradition and global chess.

עב — Hebrew

Analytical acuity and the constant pursuit of tactical perfection across generations.

عر — Arabic

During the Islamic Golden Age, the Arabic-speaking world created the literary and poetic legacy of Shatranj, writing the first formal treatises on chess problems (mansubat) and connecting the game with philosophy and sacred geometry. The ancestral echo where it all began over a thousand years ago.

አማ — Amharic

Home of Senterej, the oldest living chess variant in Africa, descended directly from Persian Shatranj. Ethiopia preserved the ancient rules for centuries in isolation, becoming a living archaeological record of the game before the modern revolution of the 15th century.
